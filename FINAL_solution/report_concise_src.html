Q1aRetention definition

Retained in Mk = at least one BNPL purchase in the k-th calendar month after the first purchase

  • Purchase = value event. BNPL earns only when the line is used; it is the only behaviour in the file.
  • Monthly = billing cycle. The median user buys 3 times a month, so weekly is too noisy and quarterly too slow.
  • Calendar months match statements and reporting. The first month is a full month here (3.23 purchases in M0 vs 3.22 in M1 for the same users).

Tracked alongside: GMV retention and monthly churn (active in month t, nothing in t+1).

Q1bCohort retention

Every cohort leaks the same way; the cohorts themselves are shrinking

Retention didn't break. 73% of a cohort is active in M1, 41% in M3, 4% in M12, for every cohort. What changed is cohort size: the decline in active users is an acquisition problem.

Patterns and anomalies

  1. Stable: M1 is 71.1–75.6% in all 17 cohorts (chi-square p = 0.51).
  2. No plateau: ~1 in 4 active users leaves every month at every tenure; retention halves every ~2.4 months.
  3. Nobody returns: 0 of 14,231 lapsed users ever bought again.
  4. Shrinking cohorts: new users fell from ~1,370 a month (Q1 2024) to 596, a steady 4.7% a month. Keeping each cohort's retention but giving every month since Nov 2024 October's 1,063 new users gives June MAU of ~4,340 instead of 3,019.

Q1c"≥ N first-month purchases retain better"

True in direction, false as a threshold: there is no magic N

M1 rises gradually from 69% at one purchase to 77–79% at four to eight. Any cut from N = 2 to 8 gives a similar gap (+5.6 to +6.8pp). The apparent N = 4 comes from the 104 planted rows. Use the 2nd purchase as the milestone, and test it.

  • Why the raw data misleads: exactly 4 (82.1%) beats 5–8 (77.9%, p = 0.0003). Engagement doesn't peak and fall back. The excess traces to 104 rows, each giving a "4 purchases, no M1" user one M1 purchase.

  • What to do instead: 31% of new users stop at one purchase, and 61% of new users make a second purchase by day 14. Nudge the second purchase in week 2 and measure it in a holdout test; expect a few points of M1, not +9pp.
Part 2 · Option AChurn

Churners are users who usually buy rarely, and they stop without warning

Churn = active this month, no purchase next month (83% of users have churned). The one robust signal is how often a user usually buys. Limit, ticket and category don't matter. Prediction is weak (AUC 0.56), so act on timing: silence by mid-month.

Part 2 · Option BGrowth

The growth lever is new-user inflow; no existing segment is growing

Every segment shrank from Jun 2024 to Jun 2025 (light −33%, medium −26%, heavy −19% users). Like-for-like, 100 extra new users a month add as much GMV as the best-case retention programme. Back at 2024 inflow, monthly GMV is +63%.

  • Value is predictable: a new user brings ~1.85M VND GMV in the first 7 months, stable across cohorts. That gives a CAC ceiling of take rate × 1.85M.
  • Quality risk is small but unproven: 2024 volumes imply about −1.3pp of M1 (95% CI −3.7 to +1.1). Guard new channels with M1 ≥ 71%.
  • Best existing segment = medium users (2–3 a month), but moving 10% of them to 4 a month adds only +1.5% GMV.

What to do: trace the funnel by channel to find the step that shrank, restore ≥730 new users a month (holds the base) and target ~1,150 (2024 level). Ask Growth what drove Oct 2024, the only month above trend (1,063 vs ~950 expected).

BonusWorth knowing

The extract is simulated, and some "insights" are its rules showing through

  • Balance field: previous + purchase (63.7%), capped at the limit (1.3%), or a random pay-down (35.0%). Utilisation, "credit-hungry" users and inferred repayment say nothing about customers.
  • Credit control: no purchase exceeds 60% of the limit (698 exactly at it), yet 4,032 went through above available headroom. Is that a production rule or a simulation rule?
  • No onboarding waves: 25.4% of first purchases on days 1–3 vs 25.2% expected by chance; the first month is a full month.
  • Real and useful: churn risk is a stable trait (target on 3-month averages); tenure adds nothing (p = 0.90); heavy users are 38% of users but 65% of GMV; ticket size is not a user trait, so value = how often × how long.
